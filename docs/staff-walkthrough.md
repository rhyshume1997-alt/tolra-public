# Tolra: a small guide for the team

Use this guide one step at a time. Start with the right company shown in Tolra.
If a button is missing or grey, ask the person who looks after Tolra for help.

The new payment chasing buttons must be available before you use that part of
this guide. Until then, use the email options already shown on your screen.
A saved draft is not a sent email.

## 1. Ask a customer to pay

An invoice is a bill. Its due date is the day the customer should pay.
A chaser is a reminder about that bill.

### Choose when to remind people

Ask someone who can change the company settings to do this.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Find **Payment chasing**.
3. Each row is one reminder. Put the number of days in **Days from due date**.
4. Choose the **Tone**: **Before the due date**, **Friendly**, **Firm**, or
   **Final reminder**.
5. Choose **Add step** for another reminder. Choose **Remove** to take a row away.
6. Choose **Save chase steps**. Wait for **Chase steps saved.**

For example, if a bill is due on 12 November:

| Days in the row | Reminder date |
| --- | --- |
| -3 | 9 November, three days before |
| 0 | 12 November, the due date |
| 7 | 19 November, seven days after |

The minus sign means “before”. A number without it means “after”.
Use whole numbers, such as -3 or 7, rather than 1.5.

Chasers are drafts for approval. Someone checks each email before it is sent.
Saving these settings does not send an email.

### Check a draft and send it

1. Open **Approvals**.
2. Find the payment reminder. Check the customer, invoice number and email address.
3. Read the subject and message. Check that the bill still needs paying.
4. If the words need changing, choose **Edit**. Change them and choose
   **Save draft**. **Cancel edit** throws away the changes you have not saved.
5. Read the saved draft again.
6. When it is right, choose **Approve and send**. Wait for the result on screen.

**Skip** puts this reminder aside. It does not mean the customer has paid.
**Pause chasing for this customer** pauses chasing for that customer, so check
that you mean the whole customer rather than just one bill.

If the screen says the action failed, read the message and try again when you
are ready. If you are unsure whether an email went, ask the person who looks
after Tolra to check before you send it another way.

### If your screen only offers manual sending

Some screens may still say that email sending is not set up.

1. Read and check the draft.
2. Use the option to open it in your email app, or copy it into an email.
3. Check the address, subject and message in your email app. Send it there.
4. Only after you have sent it, choose **Mark as Sent Manually** in Tolra.

Opening or copying a draft does not send it.

### If the customer says “I have paid”

1. Open their invoice. Check the invoice number.
2. Choose **Customer says paid**.
3. Wait for **Payment to confirm**. Chasing is paused while you check.
4. Look in the bank account. Match the payment to the right customer and invoice.
5. If the payment has arrived and is right for this invoice, choose
   **Confirm payment received**.
6. If it has not arrived, choose **Not received, resume chasing**.

A customer's message is not proof that the money has arrived. If the amount
is wrong, only part has arrived, or you cannot match it, ask the person who
handles payments before confirming it.

## 2. Email the week's hours

A timesheet is a list of a person's hours. These emails show hours, not wages.
Each email is for one person and the last full week, Monday to Sunday.

### Set it up

An admin must change these settings.

1. Open **Settings**, then find **Email schedules**.
2. Find **Timesheet e-mails** and turn it on.
3. In **How often**, choose **Every week** for a regular email, or
   **Once, on a date** for one run.
4. Choose the **Day**, or the **Date** for a one-off run.
5. Set **Time of day**. All times shown are UK time.
6. Check **Send to**. Leave it empty to send to every admin in this company.
   To send elsewhere, enter the right email addresses, one per line or with
   commas between them. Every address gets each person's timesheet.
7. Read the next-run message. Choose **Save**.

The screen currently says emails go at the next daily run after the chosen
time, about 05:00 UK. They may go the following day. Choosing 08:00 does not
mean an email will arrive at exactly 08:00. Use the next-run message on screen
as your guide.

Before the run, check the week's clock-ins and job time in Tolra's time screen.
If an email's hours look wrong, check that person's days there and ask the
person who handles hours to correct them. People with no recorded hours are
left out of the email run.

If nothing arrives, check the recipient's inbox and junk folder, then ask the
person who looks after Tolra to check the result. If sending is not set up,
the email may be waiting as a draft in **Approvals**. A draft has not gone out.

To stop future runs, turn **Timesheet e-mails** off and choose **Save**.

## 3. Work out a quote price

Cost means what the work costs the shop. Price means what you ask the customer
to pay. Check the price before you send a quote.

1. Open **Quotes**, then **New quote**.
2. Choose the right customer and fill in the quote details.
3. If you see **Price from costing engine**, use it to work out a price.
   This is the price calculator.
4. Choose the **Part**. Check its number and description.
5. Enter **Quantity**: the number of parts to make. Use a whole number above zero.
6. Check **Margin %** with the person who sets prices. Margin is the share of
   the selling price left after the cost. It is not the same as adding that
   percentage to the cost. For example, a £100 cost at 20% margin gives a
   £125 price, before VAT.
7. Choose **Calculate**.
8. Read the costs shown. Check the material, machine time, labour and other costs.
   Check the price for one part and the total for all the parts. The line total
   shown here is before VAT.
9. Choose **Add to quote**. This copies the description, quantity and price into
   the quote.
10. Check the quote lines, quantities, **Unit price** and VAT. If you change the
    part, quantity or margin in the calculator, calculate again before adding.
    Adding again can make another line, so check for a repeated line.
11. Choose **Save Quote**. This saves a draft; it does not send it to the customer.

If the calculator is missing or says it is unavailable, enter the agreed price
in **Unit price** instead. Ask the person who sets prices if you are unsure.
Do not use zero just to get past a missing price.

## Quick checks before you leave

- A reminder is only sent after approval, or after you send it yourself.
- “Customer says paid” means check the bank, not “paid”.
- Timesheet emails contain hours and go to the addresses you chose.
- A saved quote is still a draft. Check it before sending it.
